# Shifting Day: gameplay systems and level design

Keep it fun and simple: two pedals, a handbrake, four gears. Everything else is a small idea that rides
on top of driving well with a loaded truck.

## The truck

- **Gears and handbrake** (the core skill): low gears for hills, change up on the shift lights, handbrake on
  at gates and when parking.
- **Rope** (R while packing): tie things down, three ropes.
- **Anchor crossbow** (after RV There Yet's winch climbs): it rides on a turret atop the truck's roll bar, and its
  cable runs down to a drum built into the bed's headboard. Aim with the mouse (a dotted arc shows the
  flight) and click to fire. The bolt flies under gravity and bites into the ground where it lands, cliff
  faces included. The cable acts as a rope: slack, it hangs; taut, it holds the truck. Hold **W** to reel in
  and haul the truck up, and right-click to let go. It lets go by itself once fully wound in. The pull
  comes from low at the front, through the bull bar, the way a winch pulls a truck up a slope.
- **Cliffs** (one per leg): a wall too steep to drive (about 57°), then a plateau and a gentle way down. You
  cannot cross them without the crossbow; the first one on each leg explains it.
- **Fuel**: drains with the throttle, faster at high revs, so smooth driving and early upshifts are rewarded.
  Red cans by the road add 40%, and every stop fills the tank. Running dry doesn't end the run: the engine
  coughs along at about a third of its power until the next can.

## Bubble wrap and the stops

- No coins (they make it feel like an endless runner). Instead, somebody else is moving today too, somewhere
  up the road, and rolls of **bubble wrap** keep blowing off their van. They lie where the road gets
  tricky: just past a hill's crest, at the bottom of a dip, beyond a run of bumps. Drive over one and it
  pops. Perfect parking earns a roll as well.
- **Lost & Found** at every checkpoint: the stop packs what fell off on that leg back up for you (3 rolls),
  and the cat always finds its own way back for free. Broken things can be wrapped up and saved (2 rolls).
  Whatever is found or saved goes back on the truck for the next leg.
- This follows the advice on item-loss design: a loss should sting but be recoverable, because
  unrecoverable losses snowball into frustration.

## Level design

Each mechanic follows **teach → test → twist**, with **tension and release** in between:

| Leg | Teach | Test | Release |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Old Street | gears, handbrake at toll gates; the first cliff near the end | bumps, first hills | the fuel stop |
| 2 Maple Valley | the crossbow on a cliff | three big hills, toll gates on crests | waterfall views, bubble wrap past the crests |
| 3 Frostpeak Pass | ice: braking takes longer | washboards on ice, a cliff on ice | the aurora over the lake |
| 4 Neon Town | level crossings | bumps between rail crossings, rain | the neon street |
| 5 Coast Road | everything together | a cliff road, big hills, gates | the beach and the new house |

Fuel cans come after the hardest stretches (release), and bubble wrap lies on the tricky spots (temptation).

## Ideas for later

- A trailer for bulky things, with its own sway.
- Weather that changes grip mid-leg (a rain shower on the coast).
- Stop shop: spend bubble wrap on an extra rope, or wrap a fragile thing before a rough leg.
